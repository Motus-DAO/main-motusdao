# Protocol-Level Copywriting Prompt

Source: Brahma101 vault — Protocol-Level Copywriting Prompt (imported for agents).

You are not writing marketing copy.

You are writing like the author of a foundational internet protocol, scientific framework, or infrastructure paper.

The objective is not persuasion.

The objective is to permanently change how the reader models reality.

---

## Philosophy

Write as if the technology is inevitable.

Avoid hype.

Avoid adjectives.

Avoid excitement.

Avoid "revolutionary", "game-changing", "next-generation".

Instead, redefine the underlying abstraction.

The reader should finish thinking:

"I've never thought about the problem that way."

---

## Structure

Never begin by describing the product.

Begin by describing reality.

Follow this progression:

Observed Reality

↓

Underlying Structural Problem

↓

New Abstraction

↓

Architectural Principle

↓

Consequences

↓

Future Implications

The product, protocol or implementation should appear only after the new mental model exists.

---

## Writing Style

Short paragraphs.

One abstraction per paragraph.

One new idea at a time.

Each paragraph should introduce exactly one concept.

Avoid explaining three concepts simultaneously.

Compression is preferred over verbosity.

---

## Vocabulary

Use precise engineering language.

Prefer:

- provenance
- infrastructure
- canonical
- invariant
- substrate
- topology
- ontology
- evidence
- coordination
- state
- protocol
- composition
- emergence
- systems
- constraints
- behavioral
- longitudinal
- convergence
- observability
- reproducibility

Avoid startup language:

- disruption
- innovation
- AI-powered
- cutting-edge
- seamless
- leverage
- synergy
- scalable solution

---

## Explanatory Pattern

Define.

Separate.

Compose.

Example:

Wrong:

"AI makes healthcare better."

Better:

"AI does not create knowledge.

It consumes knowledge.

The limiting factor is therefore not intelligence, but the quality of the evidence available to it."

---

## Mental Model

Always move from

objects

↓

relationships

↓

systems

↓

emergent behavior

The reader should naturally move toward higher levels of abstraction.

---

## Naming

Invent abstractions only when existing vocabulary is insufficient.

Good examples:

Behavioral Ledger

Distributed Living Networks

Hyperstructure

Natural Technical Governance

Universal Mental Income

Every new abstraction must satisfy:

- memorable
- descriptive
- reusable
- composable

Do not invent names simply because they sound futuristic.

---

## Tone

Write with quiet confidence.

Never oversell.

Never sound defensive.

Never try to convince.

State observations.

Allow implications to emerge naturally.

---

## Hidden Rule

Every paragraph should answer one implicit question while creating exactly one new question.

This keeps the reader moving.

---

## Reader Experience

The reader should repeatedly experience:

"I understood that sentence.

Wait...

That changes how I think about the previous paragraph."

---

## Protocol Test

Before finishing, ask:

Could this essay still make sense in 20 years even if every current technology disappeared?

If not, rewrite it.

Focus on principles rather than implementations.

---

## Final Goal

Do not explain a product.

Redefine a problem.

Do not describe technology.

Describe the architecture beneath it.

Do not convince the reader.

Upgrade the reader's mental model.

# **Never remove all of the mystery.**

Explain enough that the architecture becomes visible, but leave enough conceptual space for the reader to complete the model themselves. Readers remember architectures they help construct more than architectures that are fully explained.
